## Points of uncertainty

### Chapter 1

- **1.2, "since 1975"** (`zemp2026`): the abstract and key points say "since 1975", while
  Fig. 1 covers hydrological years 1976 to 2025. The text keeps the source's wording and states
  the inconsistency.
- **1.4 and 1.5, Zemp uncertainty level**: the page does not say whether ± values are 1σ or 95%.
- **1.2 and 1.5, ESSD values**: 9,179 ± 621 Gt, 25.3 ± 1.7 mm and the 41% and 6% shares are the
  ESSD paper's results for its series to 2024. They are cited to the paper and presented as the
  paper's results, not as values from dataset version 2026-02-10. Only Zemp et al. (who use
  version 2026-02-10) are cited for 2025 values. The stage 4 chart must use version 2026-02-10
  and will not match the ESSD totals exactly.
- **1.3.2, SROCC scope**: "eleven high mountain regions" follows SROCC (p. 142 and Table 2A.1),
  which excludes Antarctica, Greenland, the Canadian and Russian Arctic, and Svalbard. GlaMBIE
  "Central Europe" and SROCC "European Alps and Pyrenees" are different region definitions; the
  text uses each source's name.
- **1.5, SROCC sea level**: 0.34 ± 0.07 mm SLE per year for 2006-2015 is the explicit
  "11 Mountain regions" row of Table 2A.1 (p. 202). No calibrated term is attached in the table,
  so none is used.
- **1.5, WMO 21%**: WMO (p. 17) gives about 21% of total sea-level rise from glacier ice loss
  over 1993-2018, from its own endnote 27. It covers all glaciers and a different period from
  GlaMBIE's "about 20%" for 2002-2021. Both are given separately.
- **1.4, WMO**: the three facts are confirmed in the report (pp. 5 and 16). Added from p. 16:
  1950-2025 record, less negative than 2022/2023, and data not final (155 of about 170 glaciers
  reported). The AR6 quotations on pp. 17 and 26 of the WMO report are not used, because AR6 is
  not an approved source.
- **1.5, ice-sheet comparison** (`glambie2025`): the period for "about 18% larger than
  Greenland" was not confirmed; no period is attached.
- **1.3.1, regional signs and names**: GlaMBIE gives negative values; the text writes "a loss of
  ...". Arctic Canada North and South are merged as "Arctic Canada (about 20%)", as in the
  article text.
- **1.3.2, TODO_FIGURE**: the planned regional figure uses WGMS values; the prose gives GlaMBIE
  and SROCC values. The caption must say they differ.

### Chapter 2

- **2.1.1, dam types**: SROCC (p. 161) discusses outbursts from moraine-dammed lakes and floods
  when ice dams from advancing glaciers breach. The sentence "lakes can be held back by moraines
  or by glacier ice, and both types have produced floods" is a summary of that passage. SROCC has
  no formal GLOF definition in the chapter (its glossary is a separate annex, not an approved
  source).
- **2.1.1, Himalaya trend**: SROCC reports one study (Veh et al., 2019) finding no increase in
  GLOFs since the late 1980s. The text says SROCC "reports a study"; it is not an SROCC
  assessment finding.
- **2.1.2, deaths**: "over the past two centuries" and the totals (400; 5,745; 6,300) are from
  SROCC p. 163, which cites Carrivick and Tweed (2016) and notes that a few large events dominate.
- **2.2.1, glacier collapse**: SROCC's collapse type (10 to 100 million m³) is described in 2.2,
  separately from Blatten. The chapter does not classify Blatten under that type and does not
  carry SROCC's attribution remarks on Kolka or Aru over to Blatten.
- **2.2.2, cascades**: the SROCC projection on cascades in places with no earlier record and the
  Blatten and Thame sentences are kept in separate sentences with no connecting claim.
- **2.3.1, Blatten volumes**: more than 9 million m³ (`egu26-19777`) and approximately 10 million
  m³ (`egu26-6599`) are both reported, not reconciled.
- **2.3.2, fatality**: one death (`egu26-19777`); avoided loss of life (`egu26-6599`). Both
  reported as stated.
- **2.3.1, lake**: "flooded further parts of the village" is a close reading of "damaged
  additional parts" (`egu26-19777`).
- **2.3.3, attribution**: neither abstract states a finding on climate change.
- **2.4.1, lake identity**: the press release's lake "at 4,900 metres" is taken to be Upper
  Ngole Cho, because both sources describe it as the lake hit first by the avalanche.
- **2.4.1, volumes**: 156,000, 303,000 and 459,000 m³ are from the press release only.
- **2.4.2, damage**: record (farmland, hotels, schools, hydropower facilities destroyed) and press
  release (school, health post, bridge, hydropower plant damaged; 25 homes destroyed) are merged
  as "destroyed or damaged".
- **2.4.3, attribution**: "climate-driven" is a quote from co-author Sudan Bikash Maharjan in the
  press release, attributed to him.
- **2.4.3, spelling**: "Rindhi Cho" (record) is used; the press release caption has "Rhindi Cho".
- **2.1.3, regional figures**: 25,000 lakes, 90 GLOFs and five Everest-region GLOFs are from the
  press release only. The 65% and 0.28 °C per decade figures in the press release are not used,
  because no period or source is given.

### Chapter 3

- **3.1, information gap**: the SROCC sentence on missing weather and climate information
  (p. 163) sits in a paragraph assessed as *limited evidence, high agreement* on vulnerability.
  The chapter does not attach that term to the information-gap sentence.
- **3.2.1, Laguna 513**: "installed after 2010" renders SROCC's "subsequently" (Box 2.4, p. 164).
- **3.2.2, Blatten monitoring**: only Bellwald's credit to the authorities' monitoring and
  mitigation is available.
- **3.2.3, Thame warning**: neither the press release nor the study record says whether a
  warning existed. The full study was not read.
- **3.3.2, recurrence intervals**: 2 and 16 years for snow avalanches and floods are kept
  unpaired, as in the source.
- **3.3.2 and 3.4.2, Blatten 2.0**: the abstract does not name the responsible authority.
- **3.4.1, measures**: combines the record's and the press release's recommendations.
- **3.4.3, Kedarnath**: SROCC example (p. 164), cited as SROCC's account.
- **3.5**: the report's own short comparison of the two cases, from cited facts.
