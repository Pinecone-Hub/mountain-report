## Cascading event

A disaster that develops through a chain or combination of linked processes, for example a landslide into a glacial lake that sets off an outburst flood [@hock2019srocc].

## Confidence

IPCC reports state their confidence in a finding on a scale of five levels, from *very low* through *low*, *medium* and *high* to *very high* [@hock2019srocc]. More evidence and greater agreement go with higher confidence, and the terms appear in italics [@hock2019srocc].

## Evidence

An IPCC summary term that describes the available evidence as *limited*, *medium* or *robust* [@hock2019srocc].

## Geodetic method

The estimation of glacier mass change from repeated measurements of glacier surface elevation, for example from satellite or airborne surveys [@hock2019srocc; @dussaillant2025essd].

## Glacial lake outburst flood (GLOF)

A flood caused by the outburst of a glacial lake [@hock2019srocc]. SROCC discusses lakes dammed by moraines and by glacier ice, and outbursts set off by avalanches or landslides falling into a lake [@hock2019srocc]. Such floods can travel tens to hundreds of kilometres downstream [@hock2019srocc].

## Glacier mass balance

The mass that a glacier gains or loses over a period, usually a year [@wmo2026sogc]. It is commonly given as the change in thickness averaged over the glacier area, in metres water equivalent [@wmo2026sogc].

## Glaciological method

The estimation of glacier mass balance from measurements made on the glacier itself [@hock2019srocc]. It gives annual values but only for a small sample of glaciers [@dussaillant2025essd].

## Hydrological year

The twelve-month period used to report glacier mass balance. For its reference glaciers, WMO uses September to August [@wmo2026sogc].

## Likelihood

The IPCC terms for the assessed probability of an outcome [@hock2019srocc]. Those used in this report are *very likely* (90 to 100% probability) and *likely* (66 to 100% probability) [@hock2019srocc]. They are set in italics [@hock2019srocc].

## Metre water equivalent (m w.e.)

The unit for glacier mass balance. One metre water equivalent corresponds to about one tonne of mass per square metre of glacier surface [@wmo2026sogc].

## Moraine

Debris left behind by a retreating glacier [@hock2019srocc]. Moraines can dam glacial lakes, and they can slide, collapse or turn into debris flows [@hock2019srocc].

## Reference glaciers

A global set of glaciers with more than 30 years of continuous field measurements, which WMO uses to track annual glacier mass balance [@wmo2026sogc].

## Rock fall and rock avalanche

A rock fall is the detachment and fall of rock from a steep slope [@hock2019srocc]. A rock avalanche involves a larger volume of rock [@hock2019srocc].
