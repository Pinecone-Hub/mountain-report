## How much ice have the world's glaciers lost?

From 2000 to 2023, glaciers lost on average 273 ± 16 Gt of mass per year, a total of 6,542 ± 387 Gt [@glambie2025]. This is about 5% of their mass in 2000 [@glambie2025]. In the hydrological year 2025 alone, they lost 408 ± 132 Gt [@zemp2026]. See sections 1.3 and 1.4.

## How do scientists measure glacier loss?

Field teams measure the mass balance of individual glaciers each year, but only for about 500 glaciers [@dussaillant2025essd]. Satellites and aircraft map changes in glacier surface height for about 96% of glaciers, usually over several years [@dussaillant2025essd]. Other methods include satellite altimetry and gravimetry [@glambie2025]. The WGMS combines field and survey data to estimate annual change for every glacier [@wgms2026amce; @dussaillant2025essd]. See section 1.1.

## Do melting mountain glaciers raise sea level?

Yes. Glacier loss raised global mean sea level by 18 ± 1 mm from 2000 to 2023 [@glambie2025]. For the eleven high mountain regions assessed by SROCC, the contribution was 0.34 ± 0.07 mm per year in 2006 to 2015 [@hock2019srocc]. See section 1.5.

## What is a glacial lake outburst flood?

It is a flood caused by the outburst of a glacial lake [@hock2019srocc]. SROCC discusses lakes dammed by moraines and by glacier ice, and outbursts set off by avalanches or landslides falling into a lake [@hock2019srocc]. At Thame in 2024, for example, the moraine dams of two lakes gave way one after the other [@icimod2025thame]. Such floods have been recorded in most mountain ranges with glaciers and can reach tens to hundreds of kilometres downstream [@hock2019srocc]. Glacial lakes have grown in number and area in most regions (*high confidence*), but there is only *limited evidence* of a change in how often these floods occur [@hock2019srocc]. See section 2.1.

## Was climate change the cause of the Blatten collapse?

The sources used here do not say so. One abstract describes work to assess the role of human-caused climate change but states no conclusion [@egu26-19777]. The other gives the author's view that melting permafrost and higher water pressure in the rock were "most likely" the main causes, and notes that the roles of geology and climate are still debated [@egu26-6599]. See section 2.3.3.

## What do early warning systems in high mountains need?

SROCC finds that early warning systems need strong local engagement and capacity building to work and to last [@hock2019srocc]. It also notes that the scientific literature on lessons learned from adaptation remains scarce [@hock2019srocc]. At Laguna 513 in Peru, a warning system was destroyed by some residents in 2017 during a local conflict [@hock2019srocc]. At Blatten, residents were evacuated before the glacier collapsed, which one abstract attributes to monitoring and mitigation by the authorities [@egu26-19777; @egu26-6599]. See sections 3.2 and 3.5.
