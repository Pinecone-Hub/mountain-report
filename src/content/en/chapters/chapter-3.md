## 3.1 Monitoring glaciers and mountain conditions

The WGMS estimates rest on a small set of field measurements combined with wider satellite and airborne surveys {1.1} [@dussaillant2025essd]. The resulting WGMS dataset is open access, on condition that it is cited correctly [@wgms2026amce].

SROCC identifies gaps in high mountain observation [@hock2019srocc]. Observation records at high elevations are sparse and short, which limits the detection and attribution of changes in their atmospheric drivers [@hock2019srocc]. Knowledge gaps remain on current glacier ice volumes, among other variables [@hock2019srocc]. Satellite data help to overcome the remoteness of high mountains, but cloud cover and rugged terrain still limit them [@hock2019srocc]. Citizen science, and Indigenous and local knowledge, can complement observations from instruments and models [@hock2019srocc]. For mountain communities, SROCC notes a lack of the basic weather and climate information needed both for short-term warning of disasters and for long-term adaptation planning [@hock2019srocc].

## 3.2 Monitoring hazards and warning

### 3.2.1 What SROCC finds on early warning

SROCC finds that early warning systems depend on strong local engagement and on building local capacity [@hock2019srocc]. Communities need to know how to prepare and respond, and projects need to last [@hock2019srocc]. In Pakistan and Chile, for example, communities have taken the lead in issuing glacier flood warnings, organising evacuations and providing relief after disasters [@hock2019srocc]. In 2017, amid political, social and cultural disputes, some local people in Peru wrecked the early warning system at Laguna 513 [@hock2019srocc].

### 3.2.2 Blatten

At Blatten, the rock slope of the Kleines Nesthorn began to deform faster about two weeks before the collapse [@egu26-6599]. The Birch Glacier then accelerated for several days before it failed on 28 May 2025 [@egu26-19777]. The village had been fully evacuated by the time of the collapse [@egu26-19777]. Bellwald attributes the timely evacuation of residents to the monitoring and mitigation work of the authorities [@egu26-6599].

### 3.2.3 Thame

The study does not report whether a warning was issued before the flood [@icimod2025thame]. The flood caused no deaths, which ICIMOD attributes to the flood occurring in daylight and to its arrival in phases, due to the temporary ponding of floodwater [@icimod2025press].

For the future, the study recommends hydrological and meteorological monitoring stations [@icimod2025thame]. These would give real-time information on river flow and weather in the valley, to allow better assessment of flood risk and the development of early warning systems [@icimod2025thame]. Arun Bhakta Shrestha, a co-author, also calls for more stations that provide real-time information [@icimod2025press].

## 3.3 Hazard mapping, land-use planning and risk reduction

### 3.3.1 Options assessed by SROCC

According to SROCC, the adaptation strategies applied to floods and landslides in high mountains include the following [@hock2019srocc]. Engineering works lower the hazard itself, for example by lowering the water level of glacial lakes, shaping river channels or stabilising slopes [@hock2019srocc]. Nature-based approaches use vegetation to hold unstable slopes or channels in place [@hock2019srocc]. Mapping of hazard and risk underpins the zoning of land and the design of early warning systems, which together reduce exposure [@hock2019srocc]. Community programmes prepare disaster response, build local capacity and reduce vulnerability [@hock2019srocc].

In the countries with glacier-covered mountain ranges where adaptation action has been identified, it has mostly been reactive, rather than based on formal plans made in advance [@hock2019srocc]. The scientific literature on lessons learned from adaptation remains scarce [@hock2019srocc].

According to SROCC, effective governance does much to make disaster risk reduction possible, and it should weigh both how planning, zoning and the pressures of urban growth shape exposure and how factors such as poverty shape vulnerability (*medium confidence*) [@hock2019srocc]. Looking at hazard, exposure and vulnerability together, from a combined social and ecological perspective, opens the way to adaptation strategies that address all three (*medium confidence*) [@hock2019srocc]. Because hazards, exposure and vulnerability are all set to grow over the coming century, SROCC concludes that avoiding greater impacts will require substantial efforts in risk reduction and adaptation (*high confidence*) [@hock2019srocc].

### 3.3.2 Blatten

The village of Blatten has existed since at least 1433 [@egu26-6599]. Its records name snow avalanches and floods as the most common hazards, with recurrence intervals of 2 and 16 years for the Lötschental [@egu26-6599]. They contain no rock avalanches [@egu26-6599].

The geohazard map for Blatten was updated in November 2025 [@egu26-6599]. According to Bellwald, the new map supports plans to rebuild a safe village, referred to as "Blatten 2.0", with building land in areas of moderate hazard [@egu26-6599].

### 3.3.3 Thame

The study recommends that very high-resolution survey data be used for land-use planning and for hydropower infrastructure [@icimod2025thame]. It also recommends a long-term approach that combines structural and non-structural measures [@icimod2025thame]. ICIMOD calls for a flood risk management plan for the valley [@icimod2025press]. The study's assessment of the lakes that remain is set out in {2.4.3}.

## 3.4 Response and recovery

### 3.4.1 Measures at Thame

The flood left the ground at Thame unstable, with sediment deposits, erosion, subsidence and tension cracks [@icimod2025thame]. The study calls for immediate measures in the village [@icimod2025thame]. To stop water from seeping quickly into the ground, the study asks for the cracks on the riverbanks of the lower village to be filled or covered against floods, rain and snowmelt [@icimod2025thame]. The riverbanks on the old lake deposits should be protected and their erosion controlled, to prevent further subsidence and bank collapse [@icimod2025thame]. Check dams, spurs, diversion berms and similar structures should guide the river into a channel, starting about 1 km above the village [@icimod2025thame]. To follow changes in sediment and landforms, the study also suggests high-resolution stereo satellite images [@icimod2025thame].

ICIMOD calls for cryosphere and disaster science to be translated into policy [@icimod2025press].

### 3.4.2 Rebuilding Blatten

Bellwald describes a plan under which residents would return to a rebuilt Blatten by 2029 [@egu26-6599]. He identifies insurance, donations and special regulations for Blatten as factors that support their willingness to return [@egu26-6599]. He also notes that the plan depends on its timetable being kept [@egu26-6599].

## 3.5 What the cases show about warning and response

The sources describe the lead-up to the two events in very different detail {3.2.2, 3.2.3} [@egu26-6599; @egu26-19777; @icimod2025thame]. At Blatten, rock avalanches were absent from the historical records [@egu26-6599]. At Thame, a co-author of the study points to the need to understand the risk from relatively small lakes [@icimod2025press].

These observations come from two cases only. They do not support general conclusions about early warning in high mountain regions. The general assessment in SROCC is summarised in {3.2.1} and {3.3.1}.
